Developers in Obour & New Obour

Obour and New Obour developers: the guide comes before the grade. We evaluate what is published and verifiable, and state the limits of available information clearly.

Verification path

Follow delivery track record, post-delivery management, financial standing, contract transparency, and build density — instead of judging from one ad or title.

  • Delivered units: Can you visit a finished project by this developer?
  • Post-delivery management: Is there a named management company with operating experience?
  • Financial standing: Are partnerships or funding announced and verifiable?
  • Contract transparency: Are specifications and costs written clearly?
  • Build density: What are the build ratio, heights, and space quality?

How to use the table without turning a score into an ad

Start with the criterion most linked to your risk. If you are buying for near-term residence, delivery and management matter more than a future promise. If you are buying early, financial standing and contract transparency become more sensitive. If you care about quality of life inside the project, build ratio, heights, and open spaces should be part of the comparison.

Do not accept a score without evidence, and do not accept evidence without a date. Published data can change, and projects move from one phase to another. The guide lists the source of each page and asks you to convert any important information into a document before signing.

What you can verify yourself

  • Visit a delivered project and check its operating condition.
  • Ask for the management company name and maintenance budget.
  • Request the funding partnership and its documents.
  • Read the specifications and cost annex.
  • Check the approved build ratio and heights on the master plan.

Before you commit to a developer

Ask for a model contract before reserving, and consult a real-estate lawyer. Some clauses may look standard but carry details that vary by project. Especially for under-construction projects, make sure the contract links delivery to specific construction milestones, not just a calendar date. Keep all payment receipts, request periodic construction updates, and preserve copies of every written communication. These documents become critical if you need to negotiate or escalate later.
