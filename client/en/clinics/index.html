Clinics & Medical Centers in Obour

105 clinics, labs, and radiology centers in Obour and New Obour: names, addresses, phones, and sources. Filter by specialty before visiting.

While hospitals handle emergencies, clinics handle daily care: dental, dermatology, pediatrics, physiotherapy, lab tests, and imaging. Obour has a dense network of clinics, especially in District 1 and Youth Housing, but distribution changes quickly as the city grows.

What the directory includes

  • 105 published entries: clinics, labs, and radiology centers.
  • Specialties, addresses, phones, and source links where available.
  • Geographic breakdown by district to estimate local coverage.

How to choose safely

  1. Confirm the specialty and whether walk-ins are accepted.
  2. Ask for the license number and verify it with the relevant authority.
  3. For labs, ask which tests are done on-site and which are sent out.
  4. Calculate the trip at rush hour, not just the map distance.

Specialty concentration by area

Dental and dermatology clinics are widely distributed, while pediatric and physiotherapy centers tend to cluster near main roads. Radiology and lab networks usually have a central branch plus smaller collection points. If your doctor orders an MRI or CT scan, you may need to visit a central branch even if a smaller lab is closer.

Use the district filter in the Arabic directory to see which specialties are actually available near your shortlisted area. A district with many general practitioners but no pediatrician or physiotherapist may force long regular trips.

Verification steps

  • Ask the clinic for the doctor's syndicate registration number if it is not displayed.
  • For labs, check whether they are accredited by the relevant health authority.
  • Read recent reviews from local residents, not generic ratings.
  • Confirm prices for common procedures before booking.

Clinic or hospital?

Choose a clinic for routine checkups, chronic disease follow-up, dental work, skin treatments, and physiotherapy. Go to a hospital for emergencies, surgeries, overnight admission, or when multiple specialties need to coordinate. Knowing the difference saves time and money, and prevents overcrowding emergency rooms for non-urgent cases.
