Cost of Living in Obour City

What it really costs to live in Obour City: housing, transport, schooling, groceries, and utilities — a budget framework by household type, with pointers to current local price data.

Obour's core value proposition is simple: a working city with real services at a noticeably lower cost than New Cairo or the Fifth Settlement. But "affordable" only means something against a budget. This guide breaks the cost of living into the five categories that actually matter and shows you how to build your own monthly figure.

Editorial note: prices in Egypt shift with inflation, sometimes monthly. This guide deliberately gives you a framework and verification sources instead of numbers that expire — pull current figures from the pages linked throughout before building your budget.

Budget archetypes

  • Single professional — rent dominates; living near your LRT station or work axis can cut transport costs enough to upgrade your district.
  • Couple without children — the sweet spot for Obour's value: smaller unit in a better district, with dining and delivery as the flexible lever.
  • Family with school-age children — schooling becomes the second-largest line after housing; choosing the school first and the district second often saves more than the reverse.

Where Obour genuinely saves you money

Compared with New Cairo, Obour typically saves on three fronts: housing cost per meter, everyday services priced for a working city rather than a premium suburb, and transport options that include real public alternatives. The trade-offs — peak-hour congestion and uneven district quality — are covered honestly in our pros and cons guide (Arabic).

Build your own monthly figure

  1. Start with housing: pull three current rental or purchase listings in your target district.
  2. Add transport: compute your actual weekly commute cost, not an idealized one.
  3. Add schooling if relevant: full annual cost (fees + bus + uniform + activities) divided by twelve.
  4. Add groceries using your real basket: one tracked week tells you more than any average.
  5. Add a 10% buffer — in an inflationary environment, the buffer is not pessimism, it is planning.

Bottom line

Obour offers one of the strongest value-for-services ratios in eastern Greater Cairo. Your actual cost depends on five categories you can measure in a weekend — measure them, add a buffer, and the city will likely surprise you positively. Continue with the living guide for the day-to-day details.

Frequently asked questions

Is Obour City cheap to live in?

Relative to New Cairo and the Fifth Settlement, yes — housing per meter, everyday services, and transport options generally cost less. But cheap depends on your household: build your own five-category budget with current figures from our prices and fees pages rather than relying on averages.

What is the biggest monthly expense in Obour?

Housing for most households, followed by schooling for families with children. The order matters less than the method: pull three current listings in your target district and one school fee schedule, and you have anchored over half your budget with real numbers.

How much do utilities cost in Obour?

Egyptian utilities are tiered by consumption, so bills depend on household size and usage habits, especially air conditioning in summer. Ask current residents of your target building for a realistic monthly range, and check the utilities page for service setup details.

Is Obour cheaper than New Cairo?

Generally yes across housing, everyday services, and schooling equivalents, which is exactly why demand keeps flowing to Obour. The savings come with trade-offs in peak-hour congestion and district variability — our pros and cons guide covers both sides honestly.
